# Alex Gerko

Alexander Gerko (b. 3 December 1979, Moscow) is the founder and co-CEO of
[XTX Markets](xtx-markets.md) and the clearest living embodiment of one idea: that electronic
market making can be won on **forecast quality rather than speed**. He is a mathematician by
training who has never run a discretionary book in his life — his entire career is models quoting
prices — and, by revenue per employee, he built arguably the most profitable company on earth. He
was Britain's single largest taxpayer in 2023 and 2024, which is a strange kind of public
verification: the P&L is real enough that HMRC ranks it.

## The arc: bank → fund → firm

Three stops, each one a step further from institutional constraint:

- **Deutsche Bank, 2004–2009.** Joined after a Moscow State University mathematics PhD (BSc/MSc/PhD
  maths, plus an MSc in economics from the New Economic School); moved to the UK in 2006. Started
  in **equities statistical arbitrage**, then moved to FX algorithmic trading in the *autobahn*
  era — the years when banks still owned electronic FX and were training the people who would
  take it from them.
- **GSA Capital, 2009–2015.** GSA was itself a Deutsche Bank stat-arb spinout; Gerko joined and
  built its quantitative FX business — the desk that would become XTX. The eventual departure had
  a long tail: in July 2024 he **lost a Court of Appeal case against HMRC** over GSA-era deferred
  profit payouts (2010–2015), which the court ruled were income (individual rates) rather than
  corporate profit; per June-2026 reports he and twelve ex-GSA colleagues owe ~£22.5M. Worth
  holding both facts at once: the UK's biggest taxpayer also litigated his tax bill for a decade —
  both are just what the numbers being real looks like.
- **XTX Markets, January 2015–.** Spun the desk out; owns ~75%. Zar Amrolia (ex-DB FX chief)
  co-piloted the launch; **Hans Buehler** (*Deep Hedging*) is co-CEO since 2023. The firm's
  mechanics, scale and doctrine live on [the firm page](xtx-markets.md). In 2024 he added a family
  office, **Cromulon Capital**.

He renounced his Russian citizenship in 2022 after the invasion of Ukraine (he is a British
citizen), and XTX/Gerko committed ~£23.4M to Ukraine humanitarian causes plus a £15M Academic
Sanctuaries Fund for displaced scholars.

## Wealth and the tax record

Forbes/Bloomberg trajectory: ~£12.1B (Sunday Times Rich List, May 2024) → $13.4B (Bloomberg,
Sept 2025) → **~$17.1B (2026; Forbes #255 globally)**, essentially all of it the 75% XTX stake.
Sunday Times Tax List: **#1 in 2023 (£487M) and 2024 (£664.5M)**, #6 in 2025 (£462M); his 2024
personal payout from the firm was £682M. For a desk that studies
[PnL verification](../concepts/pnl-verification-hierarchy.md): a national tax authority's ranking
is about the strongest track-record notarization that exists.

## The doctrine, in his own positions

Gerko rarely gives interviews; his public output is regulatory comment letters, Global-FX-Code
advocacy, and (unusually for the tier) argumentative posts on X — and the positions are perfectly
consistent over a decade. Against the latency arms race (pro latency-floors and batch-auction-style
designs); against **last look** as a free option for lazy quoting (pro zero hold time); against
**PFOF** (XTX competes for retail flow without paying for it); pro transparency in liquidity-provider
scorecards. The one-line synthesis: **every position moves the game onto the axis his firm wins —
forecasting — and off the axes rivals win (speed, purchased flow, optionality against clients).**
Reading a firm's lobbying as revealed preference about where its edge lives is a transferable
trick, and Gerko is the cleanest worked example.

## The mathematics philanthropy is the moat, continued

Over £250M committed since 2017, all of it pointed at one pipeline: **Axiom Maths** (£25M+, his
schools-maths charity), the Maths Excellence Fund, the Martingale Foundation (maths PhD funding),
the Observatory for Mathematical Education, the **AIMO Prize** (November 2023: $10M for an AI that
wins an IMO gold medal under open, reproducible conditions — the progress prizes run on Kaggle),
and an **AI for Math Fund** ($9.2M, with Renaissance Philanthropy, December 2024). Plus COVID
relief (£20M+) and, 2026, a cosmic-web telescope project (MOTHRA, Chile). The uncharitable-but-true
reading: a firm whose entire moat is *mathematicians plus compute* is funding the global production
function of mathematicians — philanthropy and talent strategy are the same spend. The charitable
reading is also true: he gives to mathematics the way people give to their church.

Personal: married to Elena, a Bank of England economist; three children; lives in north London.
