# Drawdown Anatomy — March 2026 vs the Current (June–July) Memory Drawdown

**As of:** Thursday 2026-07-16. The current drawdown is *ongoing* — grade the
[post-mortem](#post-mortem-stub) after the late-July adjudication events.

> **What this is.** A side-by-side autopsy of 2026's two semis-relevant drawdowns: the
> **February→March macro panic** (bottom Mar 27–30, VIX 31.05) and the **June-22-onward
> memory-epicentered drawdown** the desk is living through now. The point is not trivia — the
> March analog is the bull's favorite template ("buy the flush, get April again"), so the
> comparison's job is to state precisely where the analogy holds and where it breaks.

## The core finding: the two drawdowns *invert* fear and risk

**March was maximum fear with low thesis-risk.** On March 27 — VIX 31, 70% Iran odds, a new
tariff regime, "AI kills white-collar work" on every front page — nothing about the *semis cycle*
had changed: pricing was accelerating, HBM was sold out, capex was rising. The terror was real
and the merchandise was undamaged. That is why the recovery, once triggers expired, was the most
violent in the index's history — the discount was pure fear, and fear mean-reverts.

**The current drawdown is low fear with real thesis-risk.** VIX sits in the mid-teens, the S&P is
near highs, and there is no panic anywhere — yet the sector has lost more dollars ($2.1T vs
March's $611B software wipe, ~3.4×) on a *question about the cycle itself*: the second derivative
is bending, the 2028 supply response just got funded, and the tape sells record earnings. The
discount is analysis, not fear — **and analysis does not mean-revert; it gets answered.**

The trap is symmetric: March *felt* unbuyable and was mechanically buyable; the current one
*feels* buyable (calm tape, blowout prints) and is conditionally buyable at best. Fear-level and
risk-level are different variables, and across these two events they point in opposite
directions.

## Why the shapes differ (and what each shape means)

March's single-crescendo shape (grind → VIX spike → V) is the signature of **forced sellers
finishing at once**: systematic de-grossing, vol-target cuts, and margin flushes cluster at the
vol peak, so the last seller and the bottom coincide — the bell rings. The current drawdown's
four-air-pocket shape at calm index vol is the signature of **supply being worked through in
tranches against a crowd that keeps re-buying**: each leg flushes one cohort (Korea LETF panic
Jun 23 · systematic de-gross Jul 1–2 · Samsung-print longs Jul 7 · memory crowding Jul 13–15)
while record creations (Jul 8) refill the boat. No VIX-31-style bell will ring on this one —
which is exactly why the desk's bounce-strength detector and cohort prints, not a fear gauge, are
the right instruments.

## The nearer sibling: June 5, not March

Structurally, the current event's closest relative is not March but the **June 3–5 Broadcom leg**
(−13–14% AVGO, $1.3–1.4T sector wipe in a session, SOXX −10.4% over three days): endogenous,
sector-specific, question-shaped ("is AI capex decelerating?"). Note *how it resolved*: not by
expiry, but by **the next print answering the question** — Micron's June-24 beat-and-raise (HBM
sold out through 2027) refuted the Broadcom read and the complex made new highs within three
weeks. That is the template for endogenous drawdowns: **they end when a print answers the
question that started them.** The question this time (turn date, HBM4 pace, pricing momentum) has
its answer dates published: **Jul 28–30.** Until then, chop is structural, not incidental.

## Scale normalization (how big is each, honestly)

- **Index depth:** March's semis trough isn't precisely recoverable from the sources pulled
  (honest limit) — the arithmetic bounds it as material but not catastrophic (SOX had to be low
  enough that +40% in 17 days only *began* the recovery; SOXQ roughly +165% May-2025→May-2026
  through both events). The current one is measured: SOXX −13–16%, SOX in official correction,
  median chip −21%.
- **Single-name extremes:** the current drawdown owns the records — SKH's −15% Seoul day
  (largest ever for the name), MU −13.18% (Jun 23) and −8–9% (Jul 15) days, SOXL $302→$156 in
  five sessions. March's semis damage was broader-but-shallower per name.
- **Dollars:** current $2.1T (sector basket, 3.5 weeks) vs March's headline $611B (software,
  one week) — the current event is the larger *wealth* event despite being invisible in the VIX.
- **In σ-units:** March was a ~2σ index event with ordinary single-name moves; the current one is
  a <1σ index event containing 3–4σ single-name days. Same inversion, restated.

## What stopped March — the checklist run against today

March bottomed on four conditions: **(a)** triggers expired (tariff certainty, no Iran strike,
panic-narrative exhaustion), **(b)** fundamentals untouched → earnings pulled prices back,
**(c)** positioning cleaned by the capitulation, **(d)** fresh positive catalysts (Intel/Tesla
14A, AMD server share, Q1 memory prints). Today: (b) holds (revisions still rising);
(d) is scheduled (HBM4 cycle, AMD's MI400 event next week, the prints themselves);
**(a) cannot self-satisfy** — the "trigger" is the cycle question, answerable only by the
Jul 28–30 cluster; **(c) is not satisfied** — record creations mid-drawdown say the crowd hasn't
left. Two of four conditions present, and both missing ones have late-July dates. That is the
whole tactical read in one sentence.

- The Roundhill DRAM ETF launched early April — its "+130% since launch" window post-dates
  March and can't be used for March depth.
