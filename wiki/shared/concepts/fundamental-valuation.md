# Fundamental Valuation & Re-Rating

How fundamental value is built up from financial statements, and what it means — mechanically and in
market tells — for a stock to **"re-rate."** Written general, but grounded in the live 2026 memory case
([cyclical vs "new era"](../../market-research/memory/cyclical-vs-new-era.md),
[SK Hynix](../../market-research/memory/sk-hynix.md),
[the $DRAM-ETF thesis](../../market-research/memory/dram-etf-thesis-analysis.md)).

## The one principle everything serves
The intrinsic value of a business is **the present value of all the cash it will return to owners over its
remaining life.** Every metric, ratio, and model below is either an *input* to that calculation or a
*shortcut* that proxies it. Because the future is uncertain, that one principle collapses into **three
drivers** — every valuation argument is ultimately about one of them:

1. **Cash flow** — how much cash the business throws off.
2. **Growth** — how fast that cash flow grows, and for how long.
3. **Risk** — how *certain* it is (higher uncertainty → steeper discount → lower value).

A high valuation is *justified* by high cash generation + durable growth + low risk. This is the hinge for
cyclicals: a boom-bust business gets a **low** multiple because driver #3 is bad — the *durability* of its
earnings is in doubt, not their current level.

## The building-block metrics (read off the financial statements)
**Profitability** — Revenue (top line); Net income / EPS (bottom line); EBIT / EBITDA (operating engine,
pre–financing/accounting choices, so comparable across capital structures); Margins (gross/operating/net).

**Cash flow — the *real* basis of value** — Operating cash flow (CFO); **Free cash flow (FCF) = CFO −
capex**, the cash actually available to owners. **A rigorous valuation discounts FCF, not accounting
earnings** — earnings can be massaged (revenue timing, depreciation); cash is harder to fake. (This is why
heavy-capex businesses like memory fabs can post earnings while FCF stays thin.)

**Returns on capital** — ROE / ROIC / ROA. **ROIC vs. cost of capital is the single best test of business
quality** — a firm growing while earning *below* its cost of capital destroys value as it grows. (The bear
case on memory, per Ranmore's Lapping, is exactly this: a "historically mediocre return on capital"
industry now priced for "extraordinary returns.")

**Balance sheet** — Book value (equity = assets − liabilities); Debt / net debt / leverage (amplifies
return *and* risk); Liquidity. **Growth** — historical & projected revenue/earnings/FCF growth.
**Risk → the discount rate** — cost of equity (CAPM, beta), WACC: the rate you discount future cash flows
by. Riskier business → higher rate → lower present value.

## The valuation multiples (the relative-value shortcut)
A multiple is a market price divided by a fundamental — shorthand for "what is the market willing to pay
per unit of X?" — then compared to peers or the stock's own history.

| Multiple | Price ÷ | Good for / watch-out |
|---|---|---|
| **P/E** | Earnings | The default. **Forward** (expected) vs **trailing** (actual) matters hugely. Useless near-zero/negative earnings. |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Operating profit | Comparing different debt loads; uses **EV** (= mkt cap + net debt), not just market cap. |
| **EV/Sales** | Revenue | Pre-earnings / early growth. |
| **P/B** | Book value | Banks, insurers, **cyclicals** — assets anchor value when earnings swing wildly. |
| **P/FCF, FCF yield** | Free cash flow | The cleanest — values actual owner cash; FCF yield compares to a bond yield. |
| **PEG** | P/E ÷ growth | A high P/E can be cheap if growth is fast. |

**A DCF ties it together:** project FCF (revenue, margins, capex, growth) → discount by WACC (risk) → add a
terminal value → sum = enterprise value; minus net debt = equity value; ÷ shares = intrinsic value/share.
Multiples are just a compressed DCF — a stock "deserves" a high multiple precisely when its cash flows are
large, growing, and safe.

## ⭐ The cyclical trap: a low multiple on *peak* earnings is a warning, not a bargain
For a cyclical company, the P/E looks **lowest exactly when earnings are at their peak** — the denominator
is as fat as it will ever get. A rock-bottom multiple on peak earnings isn't a gift; it's the market
signalling it doesn't believe those earnings last (the classic Peter-Lynch inversion: for cyclicals, a low
P/E is often a *sell* signal, a high P/E a *buy*). **"Cheap at 8-12× forward" holds only if the earnings are
sustainable.** Corroborated for memory ([brief](../../sources/deep-research-memory-rerate-2026.md)):
**trailing P/E is useless across the memory cycle** — it collapses to ~3-4× at peaks (Micron 3.1× at the
2018 top) and goes *negative* at troughs (Micron FY2023 −$5.34/sh). So a low multiple at a cyclical top is
the **norm**, not evidence of a re-rate — you must read **P/B + normalized (mid-cycle) EPS** instead.

## Re-rating — the multiple moving
Start from the identity **Price = Earnings × Multiple.** A stock rises either because **earnings grow** (the
business makes more) or because **the multiple expands** (the market pays more per dollar) — *the latter is a
re-rate.* Mechanically, a re-rate is the market revising a driver: **risk down** (earnings now look durable)
→ higher multiple; **growth up** → higher multiple; or — the deepest form — **re-categorization**, moving the
stock into a different mental bucket with a different peer multiple ("cyclical commodity" → "secular
compounder").

**Three distinct senses** (conflating them is the trap; separate them and the question becomes answerable):
1. **Expansion** — is the market paying more per dollar than before?
2. **Re-categorization** — has it awarded a genuine *premium/secular* multiple (vs. still pricing a cyclical near peak)?
3. **Confirmation** — has durability been *proven* by surviving a downturn?

**When re-rates happen (triggers):** a structural model change that makes cash flow durable (subscriptions,
long-term contracts, a widening moat); **passing a test it should have failed** — a downturn that doesn't
arrive when due (the strongest cyclical→secular catalyst); a new TAM/demand regime; crossing a credibility
threshold (beats, upgrades, index inclusion); a macro/rate shift (falling rates lift all multiples). Re-rates
go **gradual then sudden** (the market anchors on the old bucket, then capitulates), and they're
**reflexive** (a higher multiple lowers cost of capital → funds investment → can validate the multiple;
reverses on the way down).

**The market tells (how to spot one):** decompose the price move into *earnings growth vs. multiple change* —
- **Multiple expands while earnings are flat/lagging** (the cleanest tell).
- **Forward P/E *rises* as the stock rises** (a "cheap cyclical" climbing should see its forward multiple
  *fall* toward peak earnings; if it climbs instead, the market is re-rating).
- **The multiple holds through bad news** (durability belief forming).
- **The valuation *yardstick* itself changes** — analysts switch from P/B or "×peak earnings" (cyclical) to
  forward P/E or DCF (compounder). *When the measuring stick changes, a re-rate is underway.*
- **Ownership rotation** — generalist/growth funds entering a name held by sector/value specialists.
- **Narrative markers** — "platform / secular / structural" replacing "cyclical / commodity"; the explicit
  "this time is different" is the *late-stage* version.

## Re-rating in AI tech specifically
AI tech isn't one re-rate but several at different stages: **already earned** (Nvidia — re-rated, then
*earnings caught up*; ~23× forward despite the hype = the healthy pattern, multiple didn't do the heavy
lifting); **priced for perfection** (ARM ~181×, Palantir ~92× — high multiple = high bar, violent de-rate on
any wobble); and **contested/not-yet** (memory). The **master variable for the whole complex is hyperscaler
capex durability** — every AI-infra multiple (Nvidia, TSMC, memory) assumes that capex keeps climbing; a
capex pause / "AI-capex ROI" doubt is the de-rate trigger. Structurally, AI re-rating is **driver #2
(new-TAM growth) stacked on an unresolved driver #3 (durable demand vs. a capex super-cycle)** — the same
cyclical-vs-secular argument as memory, one level up the stack. **A re-rate is only *confirmed* by an avoided
bust, never by a narrative.**

## The worked example — memory (mid-2026)
Applying the three senses to the memory complex
([measured brief](../../sources/deep-research-memory-rerate-2026.md)):
- **Expansion: real but one cycle old, born at the peak.** SK Hynix year-end P/B was a flat **0.81-1.87 band
  2017-2024** with no drift; the break to ~4-4.5× is a 2025-26 event — exactly the "peak-cycle expansion"
  the bear thesis predicts.
- **Re-categorization: not yet awarded; incipient, bull-led.** Memory still trades ~6-10× forward (vs ~23-36×
  for semis broadly); MS still uses a cyclical "×peak earnings / ×P/B" frame; the cleanest contrary tell —
  one analyst switching Micron from peak-P/B to a growth P/E (Jun 15 2026) — is Micron-only and itself a
  late-cycle capitulation pattern.
- **Confirmation: unproven** (test window ~2027-28); and the segment economics cut against the secular story —
  **HBM margins run *below* commodity DDR5** (so no visible HBM premium to capitalize).

**Net: the market is paying up while withholding the premium — pricing a re-rate *probability*, not a *fact*.**
