# Semis / AI — State of Play & Outlook to Year-End 2026

> **As of 2026-07-09 (Thu).** A state-of-the-tape read: the price/positioning half goes stale within weeks, the earnings half by the next print. Education, **not advice.** Evidence & provenance: [deep-research brief](../sources/deep-research-semis-ai-state-2026-07-09.md) (run `wf_9b3dfbed-aa7`; 19 confirmed claims, primary-source-anchored).

## The question

*"The fundamentals are still strong, but there was a big drawdown — or could I be wrong?"* Short answer: **you're right, on both halves — with one refinement worth internalizing.** The reported numbers aren't just holding, they're accelerating; and the drawdown was real. Where you could be *subtly* wrong is in the quiet implication that "it was just a drawdown / just sentiment." The single biggest leg of the selloff had a genuine **fundamental** trigger, and the risks that could actually break the bull case are **forward** risks that this quarter's spectacular numbers are physically incapable of showing you yet. Those are the part to watch — not the beats.

## The drawdown was real, and it came in waves — not one crash

This is the well-documented half, with dated and verified magnitudes:

- **May 12** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell **as much as 6.8% intraday** (its worst day in over a year), closing −3%. But context matters: the SOX was **+70% YTD and roughly 250% off its April trough** at that point. This was a melt-up taking its first real hit, not a top forming.
- **NVDA itself** corrected **~18% peak-to-trough** — from a record **$235.47 on May 14** to **$192.53 by June 26**. Real, but a *normal-sized* drawdown for NVDA, which has had several 15–30% pullbacks straight through its bull market.
- **June 3–5 — the sharpest leg.** Broadcom reported after the close June 3 and **fell ~15% the next day** — crucially, *not* on a miss, but because CEO Hock Tan **declined to raise** the full-year AI-chip target against a richly priced stock. It cascaded: **SOXX −10.4% on June 5** (to $539.77), Broadcom −20% over two sessions, and the **Nasdaq −4.18%** — its steepest day since April 2025.
- **June 26** — another leg on "AI-infrastructure-cost" fears: Micron −5%+ (*the day after* a blockbuster print), SanDisk −10%, Marvell −5%, Arm −4%, Intel −3%.
- **July 6** — Micron −4.7%, SMH −3%+, a **targeted AI rotation** while the Dow sat near record highs (Nasdaq −1.16%, S&P −0.45%). (The desk's own SOXL note then caught **July 7**: the −20% SOXL day where Samsung's *record* beat got sold, Seoul −9%.)

So "big drawdown" is fair — unmistakably so for the leveraged and Korea-linked names and on the sharp down-days; for the megacap leaders it's a normal correction giving back part of an enormous year.

**What triggered it**, ranked by strength of evidence: (1) **Broadcom's refusal to raise its AI target** — the cleanest catalyst, and it had a fundamental edge (a light Q3 AI guide of $16B vs $17.2B expected, a slight revenue miss, weak software). (2) Recurring **AI-capex / infrastructure-cost fears**. (3) A **valuation/expectations reset** — a strategist on July 6 put it as *"expectations are up, and fundamentals are struggling to meet these sky-high demands."* Notably, the research found **no** evidence of a DeepSeek-style efficiency shock or a memory-cycle turn as the cause — memory actually *accelerated*.

**The refinement on your thesis:** the verification pass *unanimously killed* (0-to-3) the claim that this was "purely a valuation reset while fundamentals were fine." CNBC's actual Broadcom coverage led with the **fundamental** disappointment — "weak software sales, unchanged AI chip forecast." So the precise way to hold it is: **strong reported fundamentals, but the selloff was sparked by the first real crack in the 'guidance always goes up' pattern** — not by nothing. The market didn't sell great numbers at random; it sold the first hint that the always-raise cadence might be ending. That distinction matters because it tells you *what to watch*: the guides, not the reported beats.

## The fundamentals are genuinely strong — the hard-number half

Every one of the leaders' most recent 2026 prints beat and/or guided up. These are reported figures, verified against primary sources (NVDA's SEC filing is the anchor):

- **NVIDIA** (quarter ended April 26, reported May 20): record **$81.6B revenue, +85% year-over-year**; Data Center **$75.2B, +92%**; data-center networking **+199%** to $14.8B; ~75% gross margin. It guided the next quarter **up to $91B — while assuming *zero* China data-center revenue.** Read that twice: visibility was *raised*, and the single scariest overhang (China/export controls) is already stripped out of the guide.
- **Micron** (reported ~June 24): **$41.46B revenue, 81% operating margin**, EPS $25.11; guided next quarter up to **~$50B at ~86% gross margin**. **HBM is sold out past 2027**, and the industry's $100B-HBM-market milestone was pulled forward a year. (Honest caveat: the "+346% year-over-year" headline laps a trough quarter — but the *absolute* numbers are extraordinary regardless.)
- **TSMC** (Q1): **+40.6% year-over-year**, guided up another ~9–12% sequentially, and called AI demand "strong" — rising as the world shifts to more compute-hungry *agentic* AI.
- **Hyperscaler capex** — the demand engine that pays for all of it: the four big buyers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) plan **~$725B for 2026, up 77%** from 2025's record, and they **raised** guidance back in February. The direction is unambiguously *up*. Jefferies' Brent Thill put the bull case bluntly: *"The AI economy is healthy… the bear thesis is garbage."*

On reported fundamentals, "still strong" is an understatement. This independently confirms the desk's July-6/8 read — *"a crowding purge inside an intact upcycle."*

## Where you could be wrong — the two honest risks

**1. The circular-financing loop (the structural one).** The demand isn't entirely arm's-length. NVIDIA has committed roughly $100B into OpenAI, Oracle about $300B (the Stargate build), AMD about $200B in OpenAI-linked deals, plus the CoreWeave / "neocloud" layer. (Bloomberg mapped this web; the exact figures are directional — that source couldn't be re-fetched.) Vendors are partly financing their own customers, so a slice of "demand" loops back to the sellers' own capital. And the strain is starting to show in cash: **Amazon is projected to go free-cash-flow-negative in 2026** (estimates range from −$17B to −$28B; *reported, not independently verified — this figure errored in the fact-check*). None of this is in the reported numbers **yet** — it's a forward risk, not current deterioration. But it's exactly the kind of thing that can unwind quickly if the end-customer ROI disappoints.

**2. The Cisco lesson (the valuation one).** This is the sharpest counter to "fundamentals are strong, so the stocks are fine." **Cisco's revenue nearly tripled from 2000 to 2022 — and the stock never regained its March-2000 peak.** Strong *and growing* fundamentals do not guarantee the stock works if you paid a bubble multiple going in. Where are we now? Rich, but **not** 2000-extreme: NVDA's ~18% drawdown *while earnings rose* means genuine multiple compression (one analysis literally asks "is the AI leader finally cheap?"); NVDA at roughly 57x earnings versus Cisco's dot-com-peak ~472x; the broad-market CAPE around 38–40 versus the 44 peak in 2000. *(These specific multiples come from secondary/blog sources and errored in the fact-check — treat as directional, not gospel.)* Translation: the group is expensive and late-cycle, but you are not staring at March 2000.

## Into year-end 2026 — the paths and the tripwires

**Catalysts to circle:** the next-generation GPU cycle (AMD's **MI400** ramps in H2 — Goldman just moved its target to $640, Buy; NVIDIA's next-gen Rubin cadence behind it); **Q2 earnings in late July / August**, where the hyperscaler capex updates are *the* master variable and NVIDIA reports around late August; export-control headlines (largely de-risked, since NVIDIA already guides to zero China); and the Fed — the [regime read](memory/regime-read-2026-07-06.md) flags the July 28–29 FOMC with hike risk under Chair Warsh, a macro wildcard this run didn't cover deeply.

- **Base case (most weight): a choppy grind higher.** Fundamentals keep delivering, but every "priced-for-perfection" print risks another Broadcom-style air-pocket. Net positive, genuinely volatile. Multiple compression offsets some of the earnings growth.
- **Bull case:** capex guides get raised *again* at the Q2 prints, HBM/data-center re-accelerate, money rotates back into the leaders after the reset → new highs.
- **Bear case (the tail):** a hyperscaler *trims* capex, or a financing-loop crack appears (an OpenAI/neocloud/CoreWeave funding wobble), or a hard ROI-disappointment headline lands → a deeper 20–30% derate, made worse because positioning is crowded and valuations are rich.

**The five tripwires that would falsify the bullish read** — none are flashing as of July 9, which is exactly why "just a drawdown" is defensible *today*:

1. **Hyperscaler 2026 capex guidance gets cut**, not raised, at the next prints (the master variable).
2. A **named data-center order slips or cancels**, or a **circular-financing counterparty** shows a funding crack.
3. **Forward EPS revisions roll over** (right now they're *up* — Micron's implied forward EPS rose *while* the price fell).
4. **HBM/DRAM contract pricing turns down**, or inventories build (right now: sold out past 2027).
5. **Gross margins compress materially** at NVIDIA or Micron.

## Bottom line

You're right. Fundamentals are strong and this was a **drawdown, not a breakdown** — the signals that would mark real deterioration are all still green. The disciplined caveats are two: the drawdown wasn't *nothing* (Broadcom's refusal-to-raise was a genuine fundamental spark, not pure mood), and the things that would actually break the thesis — **capex direction and the financing loop** — are forward risks that this quarter's blockbuster reported numbers are structurally unable to warn you about. So watch the *guides* and the *financing*, not the beats. The beats are already priced.
