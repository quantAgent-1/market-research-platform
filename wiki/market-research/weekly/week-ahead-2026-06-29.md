## One-line setup
**A holiday-shortened 4-day week (US closed Friday Jul 3; the June jobs report pulled forward to Thursday Jul 2)
that opens *into the Q2/half-year-end rebalancing flush* and closes on thin holiday liquidity around the marquee
macro print.** The dominant variables are **flows (quarter turn) early** and **the jobs number (Thu) late**, with
the AI/memory complex consolidating a violent two-sided prior week.

## ⏪ First: what drove *last week's* volatility (the diagnosis you asked for)
Micron printed a **beat-and-raise** (HBM sold out through CY2027), yet the SOX fell **−7.9%** on the week and MU
round-tripped (**−13% Tue → +15.7% Thu → −6.69% Fri** to $1,132.33). **What it was, ranked:**

1. **(d) Positioning / profit-taking — biggest, best-evidenced.** The **Jun 23** Korea crash (**KOSPI −9.99%**,
   Samsung −12.31%, SK Hynix −12.47%, ~$3.8B foreign selling) was a **leveraged single-stock-ETF unwind** (those
   ETFs were ~**14% of Samsung/SK Hynix turnover** that day) — a market-structure/leverage event, **not**
   fundamentals — and it **pre-dated** both Apple and PCE. Friday added profit-taking off Thursday's record.
2. **(a) Quarter-end / half-year rebalancing — real & quantified.** **JPMorgan: ~$165B of mechanical equity
   *selling* into the Jun 30 close**; the late-June **Russell reconstitution** is one of the year's
   highest-volume sessions. After a huge Q2, overweight books mechanically sell stocks → a headwind that lands
   hardest on high-beta semis. *(Fused with (d) — both are "de-risking into quarter-end.")*
3. **(c) Broad macro / Fed — the real "inflation" channel.** **Hot May Core PCE (3.4% YoY, a 3-yr high)** on
   **Jun 25** into a **hawkish Warsh Fed** (dots 9-hike/8-hold/1-cut; 2026 median 3.8%) turned Thu/Fri risk-off.
4. **(b) Apple price hikes — the WEAKEST leg.** Real (AAPL **−6%**, ~$265B), but Apple **blamed memory-chip cost
   inflation** — *a symptom of the super-cycle, **bullish** for MU/Samsung/SK Hynix* — not a demand signal; it
   post-dates the biggest move; and the Fed's inflation case rests on **energy + AI capex, not Apple**.

> **Verdict on your question:** it was **institutional rebalancing + positioning (a+d) on a hawkish-macro backdrop
> (c)** — **not** an Apple-triggered inflation de-risking (b). The *"inflation"* worry is genuine but runs through
> the **hot PCE / Fed**, not Apple; the Apple hike is a *bullish* memory-pricing tell wearing a scary headline.
> *(Confirms & sharpens the Jun-26 note.)*
