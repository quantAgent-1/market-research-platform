# Who Actually Wins: The Empirical Record

What the best survivorship-corrected data say about *consistent* profitability in public equities —
the reality check behind every [trading-system fundamental](../../synthesis/trading-system-fundamentals.md).

## Retail / day trading
- [Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (2014)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf),
  a **complete census** of Taiwan 1995–99: in a typical 6-month window **>80% of day traders lose
  money net of costs**; <20% profit even under favorable cost assumptions.
- **But skill is real and persistent in a minority:** the stocks the skilled cohort buys beat those
  they sell by **62 bp/day**, and persistence is strong for ~1% of traders. Skill exists; it is
  rare. (The claim that even heavy traders are positive *gross* and lose only on costs was
  **refuted** in verification — so it is *skill*, not merely costs, that separates them.)

## Over-trading destroys returns
- [Barber & Odean (2000)](http://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf),
  66,465 households: the most-active earned **11.4% net** vs **18.5%** for the least-active — a
  ~710 bp/yr gap. **Gross returns ≈ the market; net fell below** — so the damage is
  [transaction costs](transaction-costs.md), not stock selection. The pattern fits *overconfidence*,
  not rational trading.

## Active management (the 20-year view)
- [SPIVA U.S. YE-2024](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us/): ~92% of active
  large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 over 20 years; over 15 years **zero of 22 categories** had a
  majority of managers beat their benchmark.
- **No persistence:** **0.00%** of top-quartile funds (Dec-2020) stayed top-quartile through 2024 —
  *below* random chance. Past outperformance looks like luck.
- **Not survivorship bias:** ~64% of funds were liquidated/merged over 20 years and SPIVA keeps them
  in the denominator.

## The takeaways
- **Consistent profit is achievable but rare** — and the winners share process discipline, low
  costs, and low turnover, not a secret signal.
- **For the long-horizon investor:** absent a *demonstrated* edge, **low-cost broad-market
  compounding** is the high-probability default — it is the benchmark most professionals fail to
  beat.
- **Costs + over-trading + overconfidence** are the dominant destroyers — the behavioral case for
  [risk-of-ruin](risk-of-ruin.md) discipline and inactivity. The specific bias that makes traders hold
  losers and cut winners is the [disposition effect](disposition-effect.md).

## Caveats
- Datasets are historical (Taiwan 1995-99; U.S. 1991-96, pre-zero-commission) and SPIVA is
  U.S./point-in-time. The *lessons* are durable; the *magnitudes* are era- and market-specific.
