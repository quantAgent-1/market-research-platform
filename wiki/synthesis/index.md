- [Is Quant Research a Science? — How Strategies Are Actually Found, and Why the Published Playbook Doesn't Pay](is-quant-research-a-science.md) — the epistemics of strategy discovery, **derived not asserted**: alpha ≈ search cost (Grossman–Stiglitz ⇒ your edge needs a *barrier you actually own*, five of them enumerated); **you cannot afford the evidence to justify your own backtest** (t = SR·√T ⇒ a Sharpe-0.5 idea found after 100 trials needs ~37 years); **breadth not insight sets the ceiling** (IR = IC·√BR ⇒ a 20-name book caps at ~0.15–0.35 however smart the analyst). **The playbook re-measured from Ken French data through May-2026** — each canonical factor split at *its own* publication date: decay 48–106% (mean ~73%), **not one of SMB/HML/Mom/CMA/RMW clears t = 2 post-publication**, the 5-factor long-short combo returned **Sharpe 0.15 over the last 20 years while the plain market returned 0.65**, and value's worst 3-year post-publication stretch was −45.4% (patience *is* the barrier — Grossman–Stiglitz in different clothes). Hou-Xue-Zhang verified from source (65% of **452** anomalies fail t≥1.96; 82% at t≥2.78) + the McLean-Pontiff vintage trap (82/10%/35% WP vs 97/26%/58% published). Science, but the analogy is **high-throughput drug screening, not physics** — with two rows no natural science has (acting on the finding destroys it; the sample was globally mined before you). Counter-case taken seriously (KTWW persistent stars; Cohen–Coval–Pastor; **Harvey–Liu: the tests lack power to detect real skill — absence of a t-stat ≠ absence of edge**). Closes with the ranked decomposition: **arena > breadth > costs > sizing > signal cleverness**, and why rules beat judgment *epistemically* — a discretionary book is not wrong, it is unknowable.
- [How Systematic Strategies Are Actually Discovered and Validated — The Evidence](how-strategies-are-discovered-and-validated.md) — **the evidence half of the page above**, from a deliberately *uncontaminated* deep-research run (24 primary sources, none of the sibling page's conclusions in its arguments, so agreement is independent). **§1 the replication war and its dissolution:** rates span **18%→82.4%** across defensible methodology (HXZ 65% of 452 fail t≥1.96 / 82% at t≥2.78, and *not* from decay — 65.3% on the original samples · **JKP 82.4%** hierarchical-Bayes ±2.8% SE, same 82.4% across 93 countries · **Chen-Zimmermann 98% of 161** re-coded from scratch, HXZ's 452 collapsing to 118 ever-significant of which 117 reproduce) — **but C-Z's own next sentence says effective bid-ask spreads eliminate the surviving predictability**, so both camps argue statistical existence and *neither claims tradeability*. Decay measured thrice (MP 26% OOS / 58% post-pub, 0.582→0.402→0.264%/mo; JKP 47%) and **largest for the most impressive backtests**. **§2 what the arithmetic forbids:** ⚠️ **E[max_N] is NOT a Sharpe ratio** — a one-year figure, so "10 trials → 1.57" becomes **0.704 on a five-year backtest** (quoting it flat overstates the noise floor **2.24×**); **MinBTL — 5 years supports at most 45 configurations, 2 years supports 7** (exact integer boundaries, frequency-free under the null, ~4% *anti*-conservative on short monthly samples); holdout ~20× makes a false positive *expected*; in-sample optimization provably **reverses** the OOS ordering under memory; **Lo's SE rises with true Sharpe** (high-Sharpe = harder to validate) and √12 annualization overstated hedge-fund Sharpes up to **65%**; and **the t>3.0 hurdle resolves to a choice of error rate** — HLZ's 3.0 is FDR 1%, their own FDR-5% number is **2.27**, Chen's bootstrap spans 0–3.0. **§3 how firms really work:** Renaissance under oath — *"profitable only slightly more often than not… the law of large numbers"* at **100,000+ trades/day**; WorldQuant's **4,002 production alphas, median standalone Sharpe 1.487**, 9.1% negative OOS *after* passing the gate; and the SEC's Two Sigma order revealing institutional validation pays on **marginal alpha net of correlation to the existing book**, not standalone backtest quality. **§4 sizing cannot substitute for edge** — Thorp's f\*=m/ab and ruin above f_c; the Brazilian census (97% of persistent traders lose; best of 19,646 = $310/day on $2,560 SD; success *decreases* with days traded); Taiwan showing day-trading is **negatively skewed (−0.22) vs +2.36 for single stocks**, so risk-seeking is strictly dominated; and the Moreira-Muir "4.86% alpha from pure sizing" **resolved as time-series momentum in disguise** (Man Group: momentumness explains 45–60%) that **fails real-time** (Cederburg: 53/103, p=0.84; 72/103 lose OOS). **§5 skill is real where instruments can see it** — CFTC audit-trail HFT: **median firm Sharpe 4.30**, persistence 0.421 daily/0.723 monthly, **not competed away** (Herfindahl 0.287→0.545); Thorp's 28.5yr/1.25M bets; while the **Fama-French bootstrap misses skilled managers 85% of the time when they exist by construction.** ⚠️ **§7's tiers were closed out 2026-07-20: a third pass adjudicated all 17 previously unattacked sources — numbers held nearly verbatim; the failures were scope/provenance, corrected in place** (CFTC-HFT figures are 2014-working-paper-only — the peer-reviewed JFQA 2019 version is Swedish data, 16 firms, median Sharpe 1.61; persistence + Herfindahl were subgroup stats; Two Sigma remediation was Oct-2023; Cederburg's 72/103 is CER-based, 58/103 by Sharpe; WorldQuant's median is over its 3,289 positive-Sharpe alphas).
