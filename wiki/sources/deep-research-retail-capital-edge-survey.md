# Retail-Capital Edge Survey

**Type:** research brief (synthesized from a multi-agent deep-research run, then hand-checked).  
**Scope:** liquid US equities/ETFs under **retail capital constraints** (low AUM, long-only cash-biased, cost-sensitive).  
**Not redistributed:** raw intermediate agent ledgers and private working notes.

## Summary

Phase-1 survey asked which popular retail edges survive **honest costs** and basic multiple-testing discipline. The durable answer: **most income-shaped stories fail or are marginal**; what survives cleanly is often **risk control** (regime/vol gates, low turnover), not a money printer. Leveraged ETFs did not produce independent alpha. Naive intraday remains negative-EV in public day-trader evidence even before fees.

## Key takeaways

- Regime-gated short-term mean reversion: best *income-shaped* candidate; net-of-cost marginal; highly regime-dependent.
- Vol/regime overlays: better as **drawdown tools** than as standalone alpha claims.
- Dual-momentum / simple retail momentum: material post-publication decay in live-style tests.
- PEAD in liquids: largely decayed; residue in illiquids is cost-eaten.
- Calendar and overnight products: mostly dead or inaccessible without futures/shorting.
- Structural “retail edge” claims (microcaps only, unlimited day trades): do not survive scrutiny as a strategy class.

## Methods / data

- Literature + practitioner claims collected, then adversarially re-checked.
- Several numerical claims re-derived on free OHLCV where feasible.
- Cost model required before net-of-cost verdicts.

## Caveats

- Results depend on cost assumptions (commission windows, spreads, tax).
- Not a license to trade any shortlist item without a sealed OOS / DSR gate.
- This public page is a **summary**; it intentionally omits private trial ledgers and account-level parameterization.
