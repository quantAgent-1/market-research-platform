# CXMT's IPO — What China's DRAM Champion Going Public Means for Micron

**As of:** Thursday 2026-07-16 (CXMT subscription day; listing/trading debut follows within days).
**Time-sensitive** — grade the [post-mortem](#post-mortem-stub) once the stock trades and the
prospectus has been parsed.

> **What this is.** ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) — China's largest DRAM maker and the
> world's #4 — is going public in Shanghai this week, and the news is the proximate reason Micron
> fell ~8–9% on Wednesday July 15. This note separates what the IPO actually changes for Micron's
> business (the funding regime and the 2028 supply picture) from what it doesn't (the 2026–27
> HBM-led cycle), segment by segment. It extends the CXMT baseline already filed on the
> [supply-demand cycle page](supply-demand-cycle-2026.md) (SemiAnalysis capacity roadmap, cost
> position, "fears overplayed for two years" verdict) with the one genuinely new fact: **the
> expansion is now funded by public capital markets instead of contingent state injections.**

## The news in one paragraph

CXMT priced its IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market (the tech board, China's NASDAQ-analog) at
**¥8.66 per share on July 14** and opened investor subscription **today, July 16**. It is selling
**6.688 billion new shares, about 10% of the company**, which implies a total valuation of
**~¥579B ≈ $81B**. The company had *earmarked* ¥29.5B (~$4.1B) for named capacity and R&D
projects, but at the priced level the deal raises roughly **¥58–70B (~$8–10B)** depending on the
over-allotment — the standard STAR-Market "over-raise" pattern, and the same playbook as SMIC's
2020 listing (planned ¥20B, raised ¥53.2B), which this deal matches or displaces as the venue's
largest ever. The deal is priced off supercycle-flattered numbers: **Q1 2026 revenue +700% YoY**
and **H1 net profit guided above ¥50B (~$7B), up as much as 25-fold**. The chairman pledged not
to sell shares for ten years — a state-champion governance signal, not a normal float.

## Why the tape cared — this is what hit Micron on Wednesday

On July 15, MU fell ~8–9% (the sharpest single-name move of the memory complex), SanDisk and
Western Digital were dragged down with it, and the 3× inverse-semis ETF SOXS jumped 11%. The
framing that circulated: **Micron lost more market value in one day (~$85–90B off a ~$1.05T cap)
than CXMT's entire IPO valuation ($81B).**

Note what the trigger was *not*: new capacity information. CXMT's wafer roadmap has been public
since the June SemiAnalysis note and is already on the
[supply-demand page](supply-demand-cycle-2026.md). What was new is the **funding-and-legitimacy
event** — and it landed on a name that was **+244% year-to-date at Tuesday's close**, in the same
week a Korean broker note (KIS, SK Hynix Q2 ~8% below consensus on HBM4 shipment pace) had
already cracked the complex. Attribution honesty: Wednesday's move was CXMT-narrative *plus*
that overhang *plus* generic profit-taking — the flow-map discipline applies (hypotheses, never
"institutions sold because X").

## What the IPO actually changes — and what it doesn't

**The change is the funding regime.** Until now CXMT expanded on opaque, lumpy state money
(Hefei municipal government, the national "Big Fund"). A STAR listing converts that into
**permanent public-capital-market access** plus a listed equity currency for future raises and
talent incentives. Concretely, $8–10B buys roughly **50–70K wafer-starts-per-month of new DRAM
capacity** (rule of thumb: ~$15–20B per 100K wspm all-in at current nodes) — which happens to be
almost exactly the funding gap between the published roadmap's 2027 level (~420K wspm) and its
2028 level (~500K wspm). **The expansion leg that was contingent is now funded.**

For calibration, the raise does *not* out-spend the incumbents in any single year — 2026 DRAM
capex runs ~$20.5B at SK Hynix, ~$20B at Samsung, ~$13.5B at Micron. The right precedent is
**SMIC 2020**: that over-raised STAR listing funded the mature-node logic expansion that produced
the 2023–25 trailing-edge foundry price war. This deal is the DRAM instantiation of the same
playbook, with a **late-2027/2028 arrival date** — tools take 18–30 months to become bits, so
**nothing about 2026 supply changes.**

## The collision map, segment by segment

**HBM — untouched through ~2028, and structurally not a collision.** HBM (high-bandwidth memory,
the stacked DRAM feeding AI accelerators) is ~1% of CXMT revenue. Its target of HBM3 volume
production in 2026 (~30K wspm of HBM wafer allocation by year-end, ~55K by end-2027) puts it
**3–4 years behind** — the incumbents are shipping HBM4 into the Rubin-class platforms this year.
More fundamentally, CXMT's HBM customers are the ones Micron is *barred* from serving anyway: the
December-2024 US rules cut Chinese accelerator makers (Huawei Ascend et al.) off from US/Korean
HBM, making them CXMT's captive demand. CXMT HBM3 **de-bottlenecks the domestic Chinese AI
stack**; it does not bid for NVIDIA/AMD sockets. Direct impact on Micron's HBM franchise this
cycle: ~zero. The indirect story — a self-sufficient Chinese accelerator ecosystem shrinking the
*demand* side — is real but is an NVIDIA question before it is a Micron one.

**Legacy DDR4 / LPDDR4X — already conceded.** The Big Three exited legacy nodes in 2025 to pivot
wafers to HBM (that exit is what created the DDR4 squeeze), and Micron's China commodity business
was already amputated by the May-2023 Cyberspace Administration ban. CXMT is inheriting a market
Micron left.

**Mainstream DDR5 / LPDDR5X — the live front, 2026–28.** CXMT mass-produces 16Gb DDR5 at a
17nm-class node with claimed ~80% yields, and **MSI just shipped the first Western-vendor BIOS
validation for CXMT DDR5 at 8200 MT/s** — the qualification tell that CXMT bits are entering the
*global* PC channel, not just China's. LPDDR5X into Chinese handset OEMs collides with Micron's
mobile unit directly. This is where wafer growth translates into real price pressure — but note
the mix: PC/mobile commodity is now the **low-margin tail** of Micron's book, while data center
carries the majority of revenue and nearly all the margin.

**Server RDIMMs — bifurcation, not head-on collision.** Chinese clouds have every incentive to
qualify domestic parts; US hyperscalers can't and won't touch CXMT (the NDAA §5949
federal-procurement ban on CXMT/YMTC/SMIC silicon phases in from 2027, plus supply-chain
policy). The end state is **two parallel memory markets**, with Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix
sharing the Western one.

**NAND — not CXMT's fight at all** (China's NAND champion is YMTC; separate watch item). Micron's
~quarter of revenue in NAND is unaffected by this specific event.

## Wafers ≠ bits ≠ dollars — correcting the scary headline

The circulating stat — "CXMT ends 2026 at ~350K wspm, within 25K of Micron's ~385K" — is **wafer
starts, not bits, not dollars**. At 16–17nm DUV multi-patterning nodes, CXMT gets materially
fewer bits per wafer than the Big Three's 1β/1γ nodes, its **cost per bit runs >30% higher**, and
its ASPs sat 5–10% *below* the majors in Q1 while it rode the shortage rather than discounting
(Q1 profit came from ASP +57%, bits only +11% — it is a price-taker enjoying the cycle, not yet
a price-setter breaking it). Bit/revenue share: **~8% in Q1 2026 (tripled YoY) vs Micron's ~22%**;
wafer parity translates to maybe **10–12% bit share exiting 2026**. The market's own arithmetic
agrees with the correction: it priced the whole company at ~1/13th of Micron's capitalization.

## The cycle question vs the business question

- **Micron's business, 2026–27: essentially untouched.** HBM sold out (contracted past 2027),
  server DDR5 locked under LTAs, margins driven by data-center mix CXMT cannot reach.
- **The cycle's terminal date: the bear leg just got funded.** The documented commodity-glut risk
  (late-2027/2028, per the [supply-demand page](supply-demand-cycle-2026.md)) hasn't moved its
  *date*, but its *probability* rose — the capacity that creates it now has a public war chest
  and a mandate.
- **The late-cycle marker.** A ~$10B primary raise by the marginal supplier, priced at roughly
  **6× annualized peak earnings**, is the textbook supply response that historically shows up in
  phase 3→4 transitions of a memory cycle (2017–18 analog: the Big-Three capex splurge; 2021:
  the fab-announcement wave). The cyclical-P/E rule — memory looks cheapest at the top — applies
  to the IPO itself: pricing off ¥50B half-year profits *is* a cycle-timing datapoint. The
  Chinese-press "pricing debate" (bullish-cycle vs cyclical-reality) is our
  [cyclical-vs-new-era](cyclical-vs-new-era.md) argument, run in CNY.
- **Relative victims are mis-ranked by the tape.** CXMT's domestic displacement lands first on
  **Samsung and SK Hynix**, who still sell large commodity volumes into China; Micron's direct
  China exposure has been the smallest of the three since the 2023 ban forced its restructuring.
  MU falling hardest is a **positioning artifact** (the most crowded, most retail-held, +244%-YTD
  name in deceleration week), not the fundamental ranking.

## Second-order watch items

- **Entity-list optionality, both directions.** CXMT is *not* on the US entity list (YMTC has
  been since Dec-2022). A public $81B champion raises both the political pressure to add it and
  the cost of doing so. If it were added, DUV tool and service access tightens and the 2028 bear
  leg gets *delayed* — paradoxically bullish for incumbents. Micron's fate and export policy are
  now coupled in both directions.
- **Retaliation symmetry.** As domestic supply matures, Micron's residual China business
  (consumer via distributors, auto/industrial) loses its political shield — the 2023 CAC ban is
  the precedent.
- **Deployment speed.** The real capacity clock is tool orders: imported DUV (ASML/AMAT/Lam/TEL)
  vs domestic substitution (Naura/AMEC). Watch the use-of-proceeds milestones in the listing
  disclosures.

No existing tripwire flips — contract pricing is still rising (Samsung targeting ~+20% in Q3

## Honest limits

- **Raise-size figures conflict across secondary sources** (¥29.5B earmark vs Bloomberg's
  ~$9.8B). Reconciled above as the standard STAR over-raise pattern (priced shares × ¥8.66 ≈
  ¥58B before greenshoe), but the final allotment isn't published yet, and the trading debut
  hadn't occurred as of writing.
- **Capacity and bit-share figures are analyst estimates** (SemiAnalysis/TrendForce-class), not
  audited disclosures. The prospectus is the first audited look inside a Chinese memory maker —
  parsing it is the highest-value follow-up this event enables.
- **Attribution is a simplification.** Wednesday's MU move carried the KIS/SK-Hynix HBM4 note and
  broad profit-taking alongside the CXMT narrative; no single-cause story survives the
  [informed-vs-uninformed-flow](../../shared/concepts/informed-vs-uninformed-flow.md) discipline.

## Debut post-mortem — added 2026-07-27 (listing day)

CXMT opened on the STAR Market at ¥49.5 against the ¥8.66 offer (+472%), touched +531% at the
midday break (≈¥3.66T market value), and closed up roughly +466–471% — about ¥3.5–3.6T ≈
$490–510B, instantly the most valuable company listed on China's mainland exchanges. The raise
itself stayed ¥57.9B (~$8.6B), the STAR record this page anticipated.

Three things the debut settles or sharpens:

1. **The pop adds no capital.** The ¥57.9B was fixed at pricing; a +470% day enriches IPO
   allocants, it does not fund a fab. What it does hand CXMT is a ~$500B follow-on currency —
   secondary raises and stock-funded deals are now trivially easy, so the "funding regime
   changed, not 2026 supply" verdict stands with the funding channel *wider* than at pricing.
   2026 bits unchanged; HBM3 mass production still slipped past 2026 (Jul-17 verification).
2. **The near-term transmission was flow, not fundamentals — and it has a calendar.** Asian
   funds sold Samsung/SK Hynix to make room for CXMT; Seoul foreigners net-sold ~₩2.9T on
   listing day, yet the KOSPI closed +0.97% with Samsung +1.8% and SKH +3.2% — domestic
   institutions and retail absorbed the entire rotation print. MU fell ~4% the same session
   (hardest-hit again = the positioning artifact confirmed live). Index fast-entry rules
   (STAR 50; SMIC-2020 precedent ≈ 11 trading days) mean the passive-inclusion bid — and the
   funding rotation against Korean/US memory proxies — runs on a published schedule.
3. **The valuation is a narrative instrument, not an earnings claim.** ~¥3.6T on H1 net
   >¥50B ≈ ~35× *annualized peak* earnings, vs SKH ~5–6× and MU ~8× forward. STAR stocks are
   effectively unshortable, so no arb closes the gap — it is state-champion scarcity pricing.
   The headline it manufactures ("China's most valuable listed company is a DRAM maker") is
   itself the read-across pressure on Western memory equities.

Same-day policy escalation: Apple and Micron are now openly lobbying the administration on
opposite sides of CXMT qualification for China-market devices — the policy branch below going
live months earlier than sketched.
