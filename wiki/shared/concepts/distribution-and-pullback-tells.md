# Distribution & Pullback Tells at New Highs

> Education, **not investment advice.** The exit-side companion to the
> [conviction-swing strategy](../../systematic-trading/strategies/conviction-swing-on-institutional-accumulation.md)
> (which reads *accumulation* to get in) and a behavioral application of
> [informed vs. uninformed flow](informed-vs-uninformed-flow.md) at the **top** instead of the bottom.
> **No tell calls the top.** You cannot predict the pullback (~97% of a daily move is noise; distribution and
> ordinary profit-taking look identical ex-ante). What follows shifts *odds* and tells you how to *position* — it
> does not classify. The reliable discipline is **condition-recognition + position-management, not prediction.**

## Tells that genuinely shift the odds — but only the odds

Read them as a **dimmer switch, not an on/off light**, and **read them together** — any one alone is weak.
**Breadth is the cheapest and most decisive.** "Danger" raises the odds of a distribution top / violent pullback;
"benign" is the signature of a constructive pause you can hold through.

| Tell | Danger (distribution-suggestive) | Benign (keep holding) | Free source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breadth / divergence** | name makes a **new high alone** while peers & sector roll over | the whole complex confirms the high together | eyeball the peer group + sector ETF |
| **Volume / demand character** | new highs on **fading** volume; churn (heavy volume, no progress); **rallies sold, dips not bought** | dips **bought**, volume **building on strength**, higher lows | any daily chart with volume |
| **Price structure** | **lower highs** after the peak; a breakout that **fails and falls back** below the level | tight consolidation **holding above** the breakout (a bull flag) | daily/weekly chart |
| **Crowding / vol / positioning** | **IV-rank pinned ~100**, leveraged-ETF AUM **swelling into** the name, skew steep — lots of "fuel" to unwind | positioning calm, no crowded leverage, IV mid-range | barchart / marketchameleon (free), issuer AUM |

**Documented — what each is, why it discriminates, and the catch:**

- **Breadth / divergence (the decisive one).** Borrowed from the
  [breadth test](informed-vs-uninformed-flow.md#the-breadth-test--the-most-decisive-cheap-discriminator) and
  inverted for the top: a new high *confirmed by peers* is just the sector trending; a new high the name makes
  **alone**, while its true peers stall or roll over, is the precondition for a name-specific distribution read.
  **Catch:** the highest-beta leader can run ahead of a perfectly healthy group — divergence is *necessary, not
  sufficient*.
- **Volume / demand character.** The [conviction-swing hold rule](../../systematic-trading/strategies/conviction-swing-on-institutional-accumulation.md)
  read in reverse. Accumulation = dips bought, volume expanding on up-moves. **Distribution is the mirror:** rallies
  get sold into, dips stop getting bought, and you see *churn* — heavy volume that produces no price progress (supply
  meeting demand right at the top). **Catch:** one low-volume new high is noise; you're looking for the *pattern* over
  several days, not a single bar.
- **Price structure.** A trend is higher highs **and** higher lows. The first **lower high** after a peak, or a
  breakout that fails and falls back *below* the level it broke, is the earliest *structural* sign the character
  changed. **Catch:** a single failed breakout often resolves back up — wait for the lower high to confirm, and judge
  it on the **daily/weekly, never the 1-minute.**
- **Crowding / vol / positioning.** From the [monitoring system](../../synthesis/semiconductor-monitoring-system.md)
  Layer-2 fuel gauge: maxed-out implied vol (IV-rank ~100), leveraged-ETF AUM swelling into the name, and steep skew
  mean a lot of forced supply is *loaded* — so if a pullback comes, it will be **violent**. **Catch:** this sets
  *magnitude*, **not timing** — crowded names stay crowded and keep rising for a long time.

> **The honest summary:** the four together can say *"this looks more like distribution than a pause, and if it
> breaks it'll be sharp."* They can **never** say *"sell — the top is today."* Treat a strong reading as a reason to
> **trim and tighten risk**, not to call a top.

## Putting it to work — the checklist

These tells are meant to be **run, not just read.** The operational run-list — *before-entry · while-holding (run the
four tells above) · pre-committed trim/exit triggers · journal-after* — lives as its own document so you can pull it
up at the screen:

> **→ [Trade Checklist — Holding & Exiting at New Highs](../../systematic-trading/checklists/new-high-trade-checklist.md)**

Same spine as everything above: you can't predict the pullback, so the job is to **not be the late weak hand it
shakes out** — your cost basis sits at the shakeout level while early buyers calmly sell into your buying (the
[disposition effect](disposition-effect.md)).
