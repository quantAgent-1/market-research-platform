# Trading the Second Derivative — Why Cyclicals Top While Earnings Are Still Rising

In deep cyclicals — memory is the archetype, but the same logic runs through shipping, steel, and oil
services — the equity does not trade on the *level* of the cycle variable (prices, margins, EPS). It
trades on the *rate of change*, and it tops during the **deceleration phase**: after the
quarter-on-quarter increments peak, before the level peaks, and well before earnings peak. By the time
the cycle variable actually turns down, the stock has typically been falling for two to four quarters.
This page states the rule, shows the record, and — just as important — states its error bars and the
conditions under which it breaks.

## Why it must be so

Three mechanisms, all pushing the same direction.

First, **the equity discounts the whole remaining path.** A cyclical's market value is the present value
of the entire remaining cycle, so the information content of each price print is the *surprise
increment*, not the level. The quarter with the largest quarter-on-quarter gain is the quarter of maximum
surprise; every decelerating print after it shortens the visible runway to the turn. The discounting
machine starts subtracting while the adding machine (levels, EPS) is still running.

Second, **the multiple fights the earnings.** Everyone holding the stock knows the earnings are cyclical,
so the multiple compresses exactly as peak-proximity becomes legible — and deceleration is what makes it
legible. This is why cyclicals look *cheapest* on earnings at tops and most expensive at bottoms: the
market pays the multiple for distance from the cliff, and the second derivative is the odometer. The
[re-rate measurement](../../market-research/memory/cyclical-vs-new-era.md#the-re-rate-now-measured-june-2026)
already carries the same fact from the other side: trailing P/E is useless across a memory cycle (~3–4×
at peaks, negative at troughs) — read P/B plus normalized earnings instead.

Third, **deceleration is the first shadow of the supply response.** Record pricing funds record capex and
pulls in substitution and demand elasticity; increments decelerate exactly when the first relief arrives
at the margin. The derivative turning is not a chart artifact — it is the earliest measurable footprint
of the mechanism that has ended every cycle so far.

## The record — memory

> knowledge, not re-sourced. Treat dates as ±a month and price levels as approximate. The *ordering* is
> the durable claim.

| Cycle | QoQ-increment peak | MU stock peak | ASP level peak | First negative quarter | What holding to the level peak cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016–18 | ~2017 (DRAM contract +20–30%/qtr) | **May 2018** (~$64.66) | ~3Q18 | 4Q18 (−~10%) | ~−40% by the first negative print; roughly halved by Dec-2018. Record FQ4-18 EPS ($3.53) printed Sept-2018 with the stock already ~−20% |
| 2020–22 | 2Q21 (+~20–25%) | **Apr 2021** (~$97); marginal new ATH ~$98 in Jan-2022 | ~3Q21 | 4Q21 (−3–8%) | ~−50% to the Sep–Oct-2022 trough (~$48) while FY22 set record revenue |

Both cycles ran the same ordering: **increment peak → equity-top window → level peak → first declines →
earnings peak**, each step one to three quarters apart. The equity never waited for the level peak, and
the earnings peak arrived with the stock already deep in drawdown — the mechanism by which "the numbers
keep getting better" and "the stock has topped" are simultaneously true.

The named institutional articulation is Morgan Stanley's Asia-semis note **"Memory — Winter Is Coming"
(August 2021)**: sell when the increments peak, not when prices fall. Its calibration matters as much as
its thesis. The call was directionally right — memory roughly halved into 2022 — but it was ~2–3 quarters
early, and MU printed a marginal new all-time high five months *after* it, inside the topping window.

## Calibration and failure modes

**Timing noise is ±2–3 quarters,** and marginal new highs inside the window are normal (Jan-2022; arguably
the faded $1,255 ATH eight days after Micron's Jun-2026 beat-and-raise). Anyone trading the rule as a
same-week trigger gets chopped to pieces.

**Deceleration off an extreme is arithmetic, not information.** Nothing compounds at +100% QoQ — that
would be ~16× in a year — so the first decelerating print after a blow-off quarter carries almost no
signal. The signal accumulates as increments stair-step toward zero, and it sharpens when *spot* starts
leading *contract* downward.

**Structural contracting can migrate the signal.** Where the cycle variable itself gets locked forward —
HBM long-term agreements, sold-out-through-2027 allocations, contracted product-generation price steps
like HBM4 — the spot second derivative loses authority over that revenue slice. The tradable derivative
migrates to the *contract* variables: LTA pricing, allocation shares, capex guides, customer prepayments.
This is the strongest form of the mid-2026 bull case (see
[cyclical vs "new era"](../../market-research/memory/cyclical-vs-new-era.md) — dampened and elongated,
not abolished), and it is precisely the part of the debate a spot-price chart cannot settle.

## Using it — clock plus tripwires, never a bell

Treat the increment peak as a **clock-start**, then demand evidence before acting. The
H2-2026 navigation playbook supplies the
confirmations: contract prices turning negative month-on-month, forward EPS-revision direction rolling
over, a hyperscaler capex cut, inventory building toward double-digit weeks. The second derivative alone
is the dashboard's yellow light — never the red. The rule also mirrors onto the long side: the best
risk-reward in a cyclical arrives when QoQ *declines* are steepest but improving, which is the same
derivative read pointed the other way.

## The mid-2026 application

Conventional-DRAM increments peaked in **1Q26** (~+95–107% QoQ depending on source), and every forecaster
— TrendForce, BofA, and the five-digit private decks — has the increments stair-stepping to low single
digits by 2027 while levels keep rising. On the two prior cycles' ordering, the clock started in 1Q26,
and the 2026 tape — three flow purges in three weeks, a beat-and-raise producing a faded marginal ATH,
−22% drawdowns with zero fundamental tripwires flipped — is what a topping-window *contest* looks like.

The counterweight is the strongest ever fielded: HBM sold out into 2027, undersupply calls through ~2028,
a contracted HBM4 price step, and a mid-single-digit OP multiple that pre-pays much of the de-rate.
Against that, P/B (~4–4.5× vs the 2017–24 band of 0.81–1.87×) says what's actually at risk is the
*re-rate*, not the earnings. Which ruler wins **is** the open trade — see the adjudication that prompted
this page: the HBM "triple" vs the deceleration chart.
