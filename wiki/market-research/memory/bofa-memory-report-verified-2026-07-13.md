## Bottom line

**The headline is FALSE; the structure is TRUE; the error is one number.** The $891.8B combined-memory
figure (and DRAM +325% to $568.8B, and the "indicator 183") **does not trace to any primary Bank of
America publication** — it surfaces only through secondary aggregation (a site called TradingKey) that
annualizes a **peak/forward quarterly run-rate into a full-year total.** (⚠️ *Corrected 2026-07-13 — see the
[correction note](#correction-2026-07-13--the-micron-numbers-and-the-run-rate-reframe).* An earlier draft
called TradingKey's ~$50B Micron figure "~5× inflated" against a stale ~$11.3B FQ4'25 quarter; that was
wrong — ~$50B is **Micron's own FQ4'26 guidance**, and $50B/quarter at ~20% share annualizes to ~$1T, so the
"$1 trillion" is roughly the **4Q26 exit run-rate**, not a fabricated number.) The most likely origin of the
headline is that same **run-rate — or a total-semiconductor TAM (~$1.3T) — presented as a full-year memory
total**; the report's own 4Q26 DRAM figure ($182.6B/quarter, ~$730B annualized) exceeds its full-year DRAM
($568.8B), the signature of a run-rate leaking into a full-year line.

But the note reads as authoritative because most of it is right. **Swap the one overstated DRAM number
for consensus and the whole thing reconciles:** ~$270B DRAM (Omdia's ~2x, not the report's ~4.25x) +
the report's own credible ~$323B NAND ≈ **$590B** — landing right on Gartner's independent $633.3B
"memflation" call. **A single ~$300B-too-high DRAM figure explains essentially the entire gap.** NAND is
fine, the near-term pricing velocity is real (even *understated*), and the HBM-cannibalization mechanism
is physically confirmed. This is not "a fantasy super-cycle"; it is **a genuinely historic super-cycle
with one number extrapolated ~2x too far.**

## Correction 2026-07-13 — the Micron numbers and the run-rate reframe

*Two errors in the first draft of this page, both corrected here (surfaced when the human asked why Micron's
recent earnings were being treated as "confusing"):*

1. **A false "Micron contradiction."** The draft listed ~$11.3B / ~$23.9B / ~$41.46B / ~$50B as
   "inconsistent, likely inflated" figures for Micron. They are **not** competing readings of one quarter —
   they are **four sequential fiscal quarters in a ramp:** FQ4 FY2025 (~$11.3B) → FQ2 FY2026 (~$23.9B,
   reported Mar 18) → **FQ3 FY2026 (~$41.46B, adj EPS $25.11, reported Jun 24 — a blowout beat-and-raise,
   +15.7% to an ATH)** → FQ4 FY2026 guide (~$50B). A monotonic climb, exactly what ~doubling QoQ contract
   prices produce. There is no contradiction; [micron-fq3-2026-earnings](micron-fq3-2026-earnings.md) is the
   clean record.
2. **A wrong-denominator "impossibility."** The draft argued $41.46B "annualizes above the entire DRAM
   market for a ~23%-share player, so it can't be right." That check used the **2025** industry size. Against
   the **2026** ~$630B memflation market, Micron at ~$41B/quarter (~$130B/yr, ~21% share) is completely
   consistent — the *same* wrong-denominator error this page pins on the report itself. Embarrassing, and
   fixed.

**The knock-on — the provenance argument softens.** Because ~$50B is **Micron's own FQ4'26 guidance** (not a
5×-inflation of a stale quarter), the circulating **"$1 trillion" is best read as the 4Q26 exit run-rate**
(~$50B × ~1/share), not a fabricated number. So the report's headline is better characterized as **an
aggressive full-year ramp / a Q4 exit-rate presented as a full-year total** than as "garbled fabrication."
Corroboration that the *quarterly* figures are real: the report's own **4Q26 DRAM ($182.6B/quarter) roughly
matches Micron's ~$50B guide grossed up by share** — so the disagreement with consensus is about the
**full-year ramp's steepness**, not invented quarters.

**What still holds after the correction:** (a) **no primary BofA note** was located for the $892B/"183"
package, and BofA's real, sourced view (Vivek Arya) is *qualitative*; (b) the report's **full-year** combined
$891.8B sits ~40% above the best full-year consensus (Gartner $633B) and would make memory ~68% of all
semiconductors — **too high as a full-year figure, concentrated in DRAM**; (c) the **structure, the
near-term prints, and the Q4 exit rate are real.** The verdict shifts from "fabricated" to **"a run-rate /
aggressive-ramp figure mislabeled as a full-year total"** — less damning, same practical conclusion: don't
trade the $892B full-year headline; trust the structure and the prints.

## Provenance — what BofA actually said

A real BofA memory thesis exists; the dollar headline is a downstream mislabeling of run-rate/aggregate
figures, not a primary BofA number.

- **Vivek Arya** (BofA US semis) is the sourced, on-record analyst. His actual view is **qualitative**:
  memory is in "a **permanent structural shift** rather than a traditional cyclical upturn," the super-
  cycle "may extend to 2027… potentially to 2030," and AI/HBM memory needs **3–4x the capacity per unit**
  of conventional memory. He calls the stocks **"extended but not expensive"** (CNBC, 2026-05-27). Note
  what is authentic: the **3–4x capacity-intensity argument is genuinely BofA's**; the dollar explosion is
  not.
- **Simon Woo** (BofA Asia semis — the analyst one would expect on a "Global Memory" note; a real,
  award-winning Asia-semis name) did publish a memory report referenced on X, but *his* cited content is
  Samsung/SK Hynix **operating-profit upside** if DRAM+NAND optimism is fully priced — again **not** the
  $892B figure.
- The **"memory indicator = 183 vs prior peaks 120–130"** appears in **no** authoritative source.
  Treat it as **unverifiable / likely garbled** from some other series (a book-to-bill or sentiment index).

**Verdict:** the $892B/+325%/"183" package is **not a BofA base case** — it is a secondary-aggregation
mangling of a real but *qualitative* BofA thesis, dressed in fabricated precision.

## The reconciliation — one number explains the whole error

Decompose revenue = bits × price-per-bit. The report's implied **bit growth** (~+22% DRAM, ~+18% NAND)
is *normal* (industry bit growth runs +15–25%/yr). The entire absurdity is loaded into the **ASP** line —
and only the **DRAM** ASP line:

- Replace the report's DRAM ($568.8B, ~4.25x) with Omdia's ~2x (~$270B off a ~$136B 2025 base) →
  combined memory ≈ **$270B + $323B ≈ $590B** ≈ Gartner's $633.3B and the Omdia-implied ~$618B.
- The report's DRAM ASP **+249%** is almost exactly **2x** Gartner's **+125%** (ratio 1.99). Its NAND ASP
  **+238%** ≈ Gartner's **+234%** (essentially correct).

So **one overstated DRAM number (~$300B too high) accounts for essentially the entire gap** between the
headline and consensus. Someone took an *already-extreme-but-genuine* near-term spike — conventional DRAM
contract prices **+93–98% QoQ in 1Q26** — and **extrapolated that quarterly velocity flat across all of
2026**, instead of modeling the deceleration everyone else models (TrendForce already has 2Q26 conventional
DRAM at **+58–63% QoQ**, cooling from +93–98%). That is *why* a wrong-on-magnitude note reads as credible:
its near-term half is real.

## A skeptic's correction (worth stating plainly)

A first-principles prior says "blended ASP can't triple in a year, so a real 2026 lands combined memory
around ~$300B." **That prior is too low — off by ~2x.** The actual 1Q26 tape blew through it: conventional
DRAM contract prices **nearly doubled in a single quarter**, and mainstream consensus (Gartner) now calls a
genuine **~3x "memflation" to $633B.** The correct decisive argument against the report is the **ceiling
test** (memory can't be ~68% of all semiconductors), **not** an ASP-can't-move argument — because ASPs
moved more violently than any historical prior would have allowed. The honest framing is not "the report is
3x an ordinary year"; it is **"the report is ~1.4x an already-historic consensus, with the entire overshoot
in DRAM."** The real cycle is bigger and stranger than a skeptic's baseline.

## The recurring pattern — hype-laundering

This is the **fourth** viral memory thesis fact-checked here, and the failure mode is identical every time:
the exaggeration **keeps the checkable structural facts** (HBM 3:1, DDR4 shortage) and **inflates only the
un-auditable forecast** (a TAM, a demand multiple, a re-rate) — here by mislabeling a run-rate as a full-year
total. That asymmetry is the tell — real structure dressed in false precision. The provenance test that caught it: **does the dollar figure trace to a
primary note, or is it a run-rate / total-semis number mislabeled as a full-year memory total?** Here the
"$1T" is roughly the **4Q26 exit run-rate** (Micron's own ~$50B FQ4'26 guide × ~1/share), presented as a
full-year TAM — an *inflation-by-mislabeling*, not the "grossed-up broken Micron number" an earlier draft
claimed ([correction](#correction-2026-07-13--the-micron-numbers-and-the-run-rate-reframe)). Siblings: [$DRAM-ETF thesis](dram-etf-thesis-analysis.md) (facts hold, the
